# Treatment photos

Every treatment now has a photo that fits it. Sources: Unsplash, Pexels and
Pixabay, all free for commercial use with no credit needed. The source of
each photo is noted beside it in `data/treatmentPhotos.ts`.

## Sharing a photo on purpose

These treatments are so close to another that they share its photo (the
`sameAs` list in `data/treatmentPhotos.ts`):

- Pancreatectomy uses the Whipple surgery photo (both are pancreas surgery).
- Hepatectomy (liver resection) uses the liver cancer surgery photo.
- Lymph node removal uses the neck dissection photo (a neck dissection is the
  removal of the lymph nodes in the neck).
- Vulvar cancer surgery uses the gynaecological cancer surgery photo.
- Male genital aesthetic and penile surgery uses the reconstructive urology
  photo.

## Using a general photo that fits

These use a photo from `public/photos/care/` that already shows the treatment
clearly, and no other treatment uses it:

- Kidney transplant: a kidney model held in a hand.
- Radiation therapy (external beam): a patient in a fitted mask at the machine.
- Radical hysterectomy: a gynaecologist explaining with a model of the womb.
- Mastectomy: the pink breast cancer ribbon.
- Robotic kidney and urology surgery: surgeons at a robotic system.
- Body contouring: measuring the body with a tape.
- Children's urology: a doctor examining a baby with its mother.

## Worth replacing if a better photo turns up

Free photo libraries have very little for these, so the photo is the closest
honest fit:

- Botulinum toxin injections: an injection into the upper arm muscle (could
  be read as a vaccination).
- Minimally invasive heart surgery: image-guided keyhole surgery, without the
  heart in view.
- Male genital aesthetic surgery: shares the reconstructive urology photo.
